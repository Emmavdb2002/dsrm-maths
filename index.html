Decision Support for Reagent Mapping (DSRM)

The full mathematics behind the presentation. A LightGBM model forecasts daily demand per test type. A rolling-horizon mixed-integer program decides which reagent bottles to load onto which Roche cobas 8000 module, and when. A closed-loop evaluator then tests each plan against what actually happened.

01 · Sets, parameters and variables

Notation

Each reagent bottle is tracked as an individual object across the planning window. A bottle belongs to one reagent option: a pairing of a test with the module it runs on. A test can have options on more than one module, which is how substitution enters the model.

Sets and indices

\(h \in \mathcal{H}\)
test types (model items)
\(j \in \mathcal{J}\)
analyser modules: c502, c702, e801
\((h,j) \in \mathcal{O}\)
reagent options, \(\mathcal{O} \subseteq \mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{J}\)
\(t \in \mathcal{T}_w\)
days in rolling window \(w\), \(\mathcal{T}_w = \{t_w,\dots,t_w+L-1\}\)
\(b \in \mathcal{B}_w\)
bottles in window \(w\): carryover bottles \(\mathcal{B}^{\text{carry}}_w\) plus new candidates \(\mathcal{B}^{\text{cand}}_w\)
\(\mathcal{B}_h,\ \mathcal{B}_j\)
bottles serving test \(h\); bottles on module \(j\)

Parameters

\(F_{h,t}\)
forecast demand for test \(h\) on day \(t\)
\(\delta_b\)
yield: tests per bottle, from its option \((h_b,j_b)\)
\(\ell_b\)
onboard life (days)
\(c_b\)
bottle purchase cost (CZK)
\(RK_j\)
reagent slot capacity of module type \(j\)
\(\Theta_j\)
daily throughput ceiling of module type \(j\)
\(R^0_b,\ a^0_b\)
remaining content and age carried into the window
\(M_b\)
per-bottle big-\(M\): \(\max(\ell_b,\ a^0_b+|\mathcal{T}_w|+1)+1\)
\(\rho\)
within-window discount, \(\rho=0.98\), day weight \(\omega_t=\rho^{\,t-t_w}\)
\(\lambda_c,\lambda_\ell,\lambda_d\)
Stage 2 weights: cost, reagent loss, openings

State variables (binary)

\(y^{\text{on}}_{b,t}\)
bottle is onboard during operating day \(t\)
\(y^{\text{new}}_{b,t}\)
bottle is still unopened
\(y^{\text{dis}}_{b,t}\)
bottle has been discarded

Transition variables (binary)

\(o_{b,t}\)
bottle is opened and loaded in the changeover before day \(t\)
\(d_{b,t}\)
bottle is removed in that changeover
\(u_{b,t}\)
bottle may be used on day \(t\)

Content, demand and age

\(R_{b,t} \ge 0\)
reagent left after day \(t\) (in tests)
\(v_{b,t} \ge 0\)
tests run from bottle \(b\) on day \(t\)
\(L_{b,t} \ge 0\)
reagent lost on removal
\(z_{h,t} \ge 0\)

\(U_{h,t} \ge 0\)
demand uncovered
\(\text{age}_{b,t} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\)
days onboard

Timing convention: removals and loadings happen in the normal end-of-day changeover, which configures the next operating day. A term written with \(t-1\) on the first window day takes its initial value: \(y^{\text{on}}=1\) for carryover bottles, \(y^{\text{new}}=1\) for candidates, \(R=R^0_b\), \(\text{age}=a^0_b\).

02 · Predict

Forecasting model

The target is the daily count of tests of type \(h\), \(y_{h,t}\), from two years (2023 to 2024) of laboratory information system records on two cobas 8000 analysers. After cleaning and de-duplication, 5.36 million test records remain. Three anomalous periods are trimmed: a data gap in November 2023 and the two December shutdowns. A separate model is trained per test type; 93 test types have enough history to evaluate.

Target-history features

Every feature uses only information available before day \(t\).

\[ \text{lag}_k = y_{h,t-k}, \quad k \in \{1,\dots,6,\ 364\} \]
\[ m^{W}_{h,t} = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{W} y_{h,t-i}, \qquad s^{W}_{h,t} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{W}\big(y_{h,t-i}-m^{W}_{h,t}\big)^2}, \qquad W \in \{7,14,28,56,90\} \]

Rolling means over short windows are noisy for low-volume tests, so a shrunk mean pulls them toward the long-run expanding mean \(\bar y_{h,t}\):

\[ \tilde m^{W}_{h,t} = \frac{\kappa\,\bar y_{h,t} + \sum_{i=1}^{n_W} y_{h,t-i}}{\kappa + n_W}, \qquad \kappa = W \in \{7, 14\} \]

Rolling minimum and maximum over 7 and 28 days, a 30-day rolling mean, and days since the last non-zero count complete this group.

Calendar and workload features

\[ \big(\sin\tfrac{2\pi\,\text{dow}_t}{7},\ \cos\tfrac{2\pi\,\text{dow}_t}{7}\big), \qquad \big(\sin\tfrac{2\pi\,\text{moy}_t}{12},\ \cos\tfrac{2\pi\,\text{moy}_t}{12}\big) \]

Plus day of week, month, quarter, week of year, weekend, Monday and Czech public holiday flags, yesterday's laboratory-wide volume, the laboratory-wide total over the previous seven days, and a high-load flag.

Panel and solo features

Tests are ordered together. A liver panel brings ALT, AST, ALP, GGT and bilirubin on the same sample. Thirteen panels are defined with anchor, core and extended test sets, plus eleven solo tests. For panel \(p\):

\[ A_{p,t} = \#\{\text{samples on day } t \text{ with at least one anchor test of } p\}, \qquad C_{p,t} = \sum_{h \in \text{core}(p)} y_{h,t}, \qquad E_{p,t} = \sum_{h \in \text{ext}(p)} y_{h,t} \]

Only lagged and rolled versions enter the model (lags 1, 7, 14, 28, 364; rolling mean, standard deviation and shrunk mean over 7, 28, 56, 90 days). Same-day panel totals are excluded to prevent leakage. The final model uses 39 base features and 750 panel and solo features.

Learning objective

Counts are non-negative and right-skewed, so LightGBM fits a Poisson model with a log link. The prediction after \(M\) trees is

\[ \hat y_{h,t} = \exp\!\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{M} \eta\, f_m(\mathbf{x}_{h,t})\Big) \]

and each tree \(f_m\) is grown leaf-wise to reduce the Poisson negative log-likelihood, with gradient and Hessian per observation:

\[ \mathcal{L} = \sum_i \big(e^{\hat F_i} - y_i \hat F_i\big), \qquad g_i = e^{\hat F_i} - y_i, \qquad h_i = e^{\hat F_i} \]
\[ \text{Gain} = \tfrac12\left[\frac{G_L^2}{H_L+\lambda} + \frac{G_R^2}{H_R+\lambda} - \frac{(G_L+G_R)^2}{H_L+H_R+\lambda}\right], \qquad w^\ast = -\frac{G}{H+\lambda} \]

\(G\) and \(H\) are sums of \(g_i\) and \(h_i\) in a leaf. The L1 penalty \(\alpha\) soft-thresholds \(G\) before the leaf weight is computed.

Validation and accuracy

Rolling-origin cross-validation anchored on Mondays. Each fold trains on everything before the origin (at least 365 days) and validates on the next four weeks. Five folds are spread evenly across the eligible origins, all in 2024.

\[ \text{sMAPE} = \frac{100}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2\,|\hat y_i - y_i|}{|y_i| + |\hat y_i|}, \qquad \text{MAE} = \frac1n\sum|\hat y_i - y_i|, \qquad \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\tfrac1n\sum(\hat y_i - y_i)^2} \]
\[ d = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat y_i)^2}{\sum \big(|\hat y_i - \bar y| + |y_i - \bar y|\big)^2} \qquad \text{(Willmott index of agreement)} \]

03 · Optimise

Allocation MIP: constraints

One window is solved per day in IBM CPLEX through DOcplex. All constraints hold for every bottle \(b \in \mathcal{B}_w\) and day \(t \in \mathcal{T}_w\) unless stated otherwise.

  1. C1
    State exclusivity
    \[ y^{\text{on}}_{b,t} + y^{\text{new}}_{b,t} + y^{\text{dis}}_{b,t} = 1 \]

    Every bottle is in exactly one state: unopened, onboard or discarded.

  2. C2
    Transition sources
    \[ o_{b,t} \le y^{\text{new}}_{b,t-1}, \qquad d_{b,t} \le y^{\text{on}}_{b,t-1} \]

    Only an unopened bottle can be loaded. Only a bottle that was onboard can be removed.

  3. C3
    State balance
    \[ y^{\text{new}}_{b,t} = y^{\text{new}}_{b,t-1} - o_{b,t}, \qquad y^{\text{on}}_{b,t} = y^{\text{on}}_{b,t-1} + o_{b,t} - d_{b,t}, \qquad y^{\text{dis}}_{b,t} = y^{\text{dis}}_{b,t-1} + d_{b,t} \]

    States change only through loading and removal events, so a discarded bottle can never return.

  4. C4
    No empty carryover
    \[ R_{b,t-1} + \delta_b\, o_{b,t} \ge \varepsilon_R\, y^{\text{on}}_{b,t}, \qquad \varepsilon_R = 10^{-6} \]

    A bottle that enters the changeover empty must come off rather than occupy a slot.

  5. C5
    Usability and expiry for use
    \[ u_{b,t} \le y^{\text{on}}_{b,t}, \qquad \text{age}_{b,t-1} \le (\ell_b - 1) + M_b\,(1 - u_{b,t}) \]

    Only an onboard bottle inside its onboard life can be used. A freshly loaded bottle has age zero and is usable on its first day.

  6. C6
    Forced removal at expiry
    \[ \text{age}_{b,t-1} \le (\ell_b - 1) + M_b\,(1 - y^{\text{on}}_{b,t}) \]

    An expired bottle cannot stay onboard. Any reagent left inside is recorded as loss.

  7. C7
    Content balance
    \[ R_{b,t} = R_{b,t-1} + \delta_b\, o_{b,t} - v_{b,t} - L_{b,t} \]

    Reagent is conserved: what was there plus what was loaded equals what was used, what remains and what was lost.

  8. C8
    Consumption bounds
    \[ v_{b,t} \le \delta_b\, u_{b,t}, \qquad v_{b,t} \le R_{b,t-1} + \delta_b\, o_{b,t} \]

    A bottle can only run tests if it is usable and has content.

  9. C9
    Remaining content only while onboard
    \[ 0 \le R_{b,t} \le \delta_b\, y^{\text{on}}_{b,t} \]

    Once a bottle is removed, its remaining content is zero, which pushes leftover reagent into \(L_{b,t}\) through C7.

  10. C10
    Loss only on removal
    \[ L_{b,t} \le \delta_b\, d_{b,t} \]

    Reagent is only lost when a bottle comes off. Waste is measured, not forbidden.

  11. C11
    Age progression
    \[ \text{age}_{b,t-1} + 1 - M_b(1 - y^{\text{on}}_{b,t}) \;\le\; \text{age}_{b,t} \;\le\; \text{age}_{b,t-1} + 1 + M_b(1 - y^{\text{on}}_{b,t}), \qquad \text{age}_{b,t} \le (M_b - 1)\, y^{\text{on}}_{b,t} \]

    Age rises by one for each day onboard and resets to zero once the bottle is off. The big-\(M\) is set per bottle, which keeps short-life reagents tight.

  12. C12
    Rack slot capacity
    \[ \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_j} y^{\text{on}}_{b,t} \le RK_j \qquad \forall j, t \]

    Counts bottles onboard at the same time after the changeover. An outgoing bottle and its replacement share one slot in sequence.

  13. C13
    Daily throughput
    \[ \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_j} v_{b,t} \le \Theta_j \qquad \forall j, t \]

    A module can have free slots and still be unable to process more tests in an eight-hour day.

  14. C14
    Demand balance
    \[ z_{h,t} = \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_h} v_{b,t}, \qquad z_{h,t} + U_{h,t} = F_{h,t} \qquad \forall h, t \]

    Demand for a test can be served from any of its options on any module. What cannot be served is reported honestly as uncovered.

  15. C15
    Symmetry breaking
    \[ \sum_{\tau \le t} o_{k,\tau} \le \sum_{\tau \le t} o_{k-1,\tau} \qquad \text{for consecutive candidates } k-1, k \text{ of the same option} \]

    Identical candidate bottles open in a fixed order, which removes equivalent solutions from the branch-and-bound tree.

Domains: \(y, o, d, u\) binary; \(R, v, L, z, U\) continuous and non-negative; age integer.

04 · Optimise

Objective: three lexicographic stages

Service comes first. Each stage fixes its optimum as a constraint before the next stage is solved.

Stage 1A · protect today's service

\[ U^{\ast}_{1} = \min \sum_{h} U_{h,t_w} \quad \text{then add} \quad \sum_h U_{h,t_w} \le U^{\ast}_{1} \]

Only day one is ever implemented. Without this stage, a window-total objective could push unavoidable shortage onto today to make a later, never-executed day look better.

Stage 1B · best service over the window

\[ U^{\ast} = \min \sum_{h}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_w} U_{h,t} \;+\; \varepsilon_w \sum_{b,t} L_{b,t}, \qquad \varepsilon_w = \frac{1}{\sum_b \delta_b + 1} \quad \text{then add} \quad \sum_{h,t} U_{h,t} \le U^{\ast} \]

The tiny loss term only breaks ties between equally good service plans. It is too small to ever trade one served test for less waste.

Stage 2 · cost and disruption

\[ \min \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_w} \omega_t \Big[\ \lambda_c \sum_b c_b\, o_{b,t} \;+\; \lambda_\ell \sum_b \frac{c_b}{\delta_b}\, L_{b,t} \;+\; \lambda_d \sum_b o_{b,t}\ \Big] \]

The two objectives from the SAIIE36 paper, reagent cost and bottle-opening disruption, with discarded reagent valued at its per-test cost. Bottle openings are the disruption proxy. Weights: \(\lambda_c = 1\), \(\lambda_\ell = 30\), \(\lambda_d = 5000\), chosen by sensitivity testing (Experiments 11 to 13).

05 · Optimise

Rolling horizon

Each day the model plans \(L = 7\) days ahead, implements only the first day, and re-plans the next morning with updated information.

  1. Build the window's bottles: every bottle still onboard (with its real remaining content \(R^0_b\) and age \(a^0_b\)) plus fresh candidates for each option.
  2. Solve Stages 1A, 1B and 2 over \(\mathcal{T}_w\).
  3. Implement day \(t_w\): its loadings, removals and onboard configuration.
  4. Carry every bottle with \(y^{\text{on}}_{b,t_w} = 1\) into window \(w+1\). Unopened candidates are dropped and regenerated.

The number of candidates per option is a safe upper bound that still works when onboard life is shorter than the time to empty a bottle:

\[ n_{h,j} = \max\Big(1,\ \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_w} \Big\lceil \frac{F_{h,t}}{\delta_{h,j}} \Big\rceil + 1\Big) \]

Generating candidates inside each window keeps the model small. A full-year bottle index would need thousands of identities.

06 · Execute

Closed-loop evaluation

A forecast-driven plan must be judged against what really happened, not against its own forecast. After each solve, day one's configuration is frozen and exposed to realised demand \(D_{h,t}\). Let \(\mathcal{B}^{\text{on}}_t\) be the bottles onboard and \(A_b = R_{b,t-1} + \delta_b o_{b,t}\) their available content.

Execution E1 · serve as much realised demand as possible

\[ U^{a\ast} = \min \sum_h U^a_h \quad \text{s.t.} \quad 0 \le x_b \le A_b,\quad \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_j \cap \mathcal{B}^{\text{on}}_t} x_b \le \Theta_j,\quad \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_h \cap \mathcal{B}^{\text{on}}_t} x_b + U^a_h = D_{h,t} \]

Execution E2 · stay close to the plan

\[ \min \sum_{b} \big(e^{+}_b + e^{-}_b\big) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad x_b - \hat v_b = e^{+}_b - e^{-}_b,\quad \sum_h U^a_h \le U^{a\ast},\quad \text{E1 constraints} \]

\(\hat v_b\) is the planned consumption. The evaluator may not open or remove bottles after seeing actual demand. The realised remaining content \(A_b - x_b\) carries into the next planning day.

Experiment 1 · 1 to 15 April 2024

Planning from out-of-sample LightGBM forecasts served 99.854% of 106,840 realised tests. A perfect-information plan served 100%. The forecast-driven plan left 156 more tests uncovered and opened 4 more bottles (244 against 240). This gap is the operational cost of forecast error for the same optimiser.

10 · What the conversion adds

Doctoral phase

The integrated predict-then-optimise pipeline above is complete. The doctoral phase builds on it with two further components.

  1. Scenario simulation model. A rule-based model of current laboratory practice, so DSRM can be compared against how bottles are loaded today under the same demand.
  2. Operating conditions characterisation. A methodology for describing a laboratory's operating conditions and showing under which conditions DSRM adds the most value, using the case laboratory as the stable baseline.
